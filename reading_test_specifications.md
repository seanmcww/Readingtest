# Reading Assessment Test - Parallel Forms Specifications
## Technical Specifications for 8 Equivalent Tests

---

## OVERVIEW

**Purpose:** Create 8 parallel form reading assessments that measure identical constructs with equivalent difficulty, enabling reliable progress tracking over time.

**Target Population:** Year 7-8 students (ages 11-13)  
**Reading Age Range:** 7-16  
**Test Duration:** 25 minutes  
**Total Questions:** 26 multiple choice (4 options each)  
**Administration:** Digital, auto-marked

---

## PART 1: NARRATIVE FICTION SPECIFICATIONS

### Passage Requirements

**Text Type:** Contemporary or near-contemporary narrative fiction (published post-1950 or written in modern style)

**Quantitative Measures:**
- Word count: 450-550 words (target: 500 words)
- Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level: 5.5-6.5
- Flesch Reading Ease: 75-85
- Average sentence length: 12-16 words
- Average syllables per word: 1.4-1.6
- Percentage of complex words (3+ syllables): 8-12%

**Qualitative Measures:**
- **Narrative Structure:** Clear beginning, middle, end with problem-resolution arc
- **Character Focus:** 1-2 main characters with clear emotional journey
- **Setting:** Accessible, relatable (home, school, neighborhood contexts)
- **Themes:** Age-appropriate (family, friendship, personal growth, small challenges)
- **Dialogue:** 20-30% of passage should include dialogue
- **Vocabulary:** Primarily Tier 1 words, with 8-12 Tier 2 words in context

**Content Constraints:**
- Third person limited OR first person narration
- Present or recent past tense
- Linear chronology (no flashbacks or complex time shifts)
- Contemporary realistic setting (avoid fantasy, sci-fi, historical unless very accessible)

**Emotional Tone:** Warm, optimistic, or gently humorous (avoid heavy themes like death, divorce, trauma)

- Questions 8-10: Require synthesis of multiple textual elements
- All distractors must be plausible based on surface reading
- Correct answers must be unambiguously supportable from text

## PART 2: NON-FICTION SPECIFICATIONS

### Passage Requirements

**Text Type:** Informational/explanatory non-fiction on accessible scientific, natural, or social topics

**Quantitative Measures:**
- Word count: 380-420 words (target: 400 words)
- Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level: 7.5-8.5
- Flesch Reading Ease: 60-70
- Average sentence length: 15-18 words
- Average syllables per word: 1.6-1.8
- Percentage of complex words: 15-20%

**Qualitative Measures:**
- **Structure:** Clear topic sentences, logical progression, 5-6 paragraphs
- **Text Features:** May include technical terms with context clues
- **Explanatory Devices:** Uses examples, comparisons, cause-effect relationships
- **Vocabulary:** Mix of Tier 2 (academic) and domain-specific Tier 3 words (all contextualized)
- **Tone:** Informative but engaging, not overly technical

**Content Guidelines:**
- **Topic Areas:** Natural science (animals, human biology, earth science), technology, history, or social phenomena
- **Accessibility:** No assumed prior knowledge required
- **Engagement:** Include surprising facts or counterintuitive information
- **Organizational Patterns:** Use compare-contrast, cause-effect, or problem-solution structures

**Topics to Avoid:** Controversial issues, abstract concepts without concrete examples, topics requiring mathematical computation

- Question 17: Focus on why author chose specific word/phrase
- Question 18: Requires understanding the passage as a whole
- Include 2-3 questions that use "According to the passage" stem

## PART 3: LITERARY NON-FICTION SPECIFICATIONS

### Passage Requirements

**Text Type:** Descriptive, reflective, or narrative literary non-fiction (travel writing, nature writing, personal essay, memoir excerpt)

**Quantitative Measures:**
- Word count: 420-480 words (target: 450 words)
- Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level: 9.0-10.5
- Flesch Reading Ease: 50-60
- Average sentence length: 18-22 words
- Average syllables per word: 1.7-2.0
- Percentage of complex words: 20-25%

**Qualitative Measures:**
- **Literary Quality:** Uses figurative language, varied sentence structure, sophisticated vocabulary
- **Voice:** Strong authorial voice, first person or immersive third person
- **Imagery:** Rich sensory details (visual, auditory, tactile)
- **Complexity:** May include subordinate clauses, appositives, sophisticated syntax
- **Vocabulary:** Significant use of Tier 2 words, some Tier 3, emphasis on precise/evocative language

**Content Guidelines:**
- **Subject Matter:** Nature observations, travel experiences, cultural reflections, sensory experiences
- **Tone:** Contemplative, descriptive, or gently philosophical
- **Structure:** May be less rigidly organized than Part 2; can follow associative logic
- **Accessibility:** Despite complexity, should have clear through-line or central reflection

**Literary Devices Expected:**
- Metaphor and/or simile (2-3 instances)
- Personification or anthropomorphism (optional, 1-2 instances)
- Alliteration or assonance (subtle, not excessive)
- Varied sentence structures including periodic sentences

**Question Construction Rules:**
- Questions 19, 22, 26: Must focus on Tier 2/3 vocabulary or technical terms
- Questions 20, 23, 25: Require interpretation of subtext, theme, or figurative meaning
- Question 21: Focus on "why did the author..." or "what does the author suggest..."
- Question 24: Requires synthesizing the overall message or reflection
- All questions must avoid subjective interpretation - answers must be defensible
- Distractors should include literal interpretations of figurative language

## CROSS-TEST EQUIVALENCE REQUIREMENTS

### Statistical Equivalence Targets

**Test Difficulty (P-value = proportion answering correctly):**
- Overall test mean P-value: 0.65-0.70 (65-70% average correct)
- Part 1 mean P-value: 0.72-0.78
- Part 2 mean P-value: 0.65-0.72
- Part 3 mean P-value: 0.55-0.65

**Individual Question P-values by Difficulty Level:**
- Difficulty 1: P-value 0.85-0.95
- Difficulty 2: P-value 0.75-0.85
- Difficulty 3: P-value 0.65-0.75
- Difficulty 4: P-value 0.50-0.65
- Difficulty 5: P-value 0.35-0.50

**Discrimination (Point-Biserial Correlation):**
- Target range for all items: 0.30-0.70
- Items below 0.20 should be revised
- Items above 0.70 may be too discriminating

**Reliability:**
- Target Cronbach's Alpha: 0.85-0.90
- Split-half reliability: >0.85

## QUESTION WRITING STANDARDS

### Multiple Choice Best Practices

**Stem Construction:**
- Use clear, direct language
- Include as much of the question in the stem as possible
- Avoid negative phrasing unless necessary ("Which is NOT...")
- For vocabulary questions, include sufficient context in stem

**Answer Options:**
- All options should be grammatically parallel
- Similar length across all options (±3 words)
- Alphabetize when appropriate (for dates, numbers)
- Avoid "all of the above" or "none of the above"
- Avoid absolutes like "always," "never" in distractors

**Distractor Quality:**
- Each distractor must be plausible to students who partially understand
- Common distractor types:
  - Partially correct (contains some truth but incomplete/overgeneralized)
  - Common misconception
  - Information from passage but answers wrong question
  - Too literal interpretation of figurative language
  - Superficially similar to correct answer
- Avoid joke options or obviously wrong answers
- No "trick" distractors that rely on wordplay

**Correct Answer Characteristics:**
- Unambiguously correct based on passage
- Not dependent on outside knowledge
- Most complete/accurate of all options
- Direct evidence available in text

### Answer Key Distribution

**Overall balance across 26 questions:**
- Option A: 6-7 times (23-27%)
- Option B: 6-7 times (23-27%)
- Option C: 6-7 times (23-27%)
- Option D: 6-7 times (23-27%)

**Avoid patterns:**
- No more than 3 consecutive questions with same answer
- Distribute answers evenly within each part
- Don't create predictable sequences (A, B, C, D)

## PASSAGE DEVELOPMENT PROTOCOL

### Step 1: Topic Selection
- Choose from approved topic list
- Ensure topic is accessible, engaging, and age-appropriate
- Verify no overlap with other test topics

### Step 2: Draft Passage
- Write to target word count
- Include required text features for passage type
- Embed Tier 2 vocabulary naturally in context

### Step 3: Readability Analysis
- Run Flesch-Kincaid analysis
- Check sentence length distribution
- Verify vocabulary complexity matches targets
- Adjust as needed to meet quantitative targets

### Step 4: Content Review
- Verify accuracy of factual information
- Check for bias, stereotypes, or sensitive content
- Ensure cultural accessibility for UK students
- Confirm age-appropriateness

### Step 5: Question Development
- Draft questions following matrix specifications
- Ensure each question has single defensible answer
- Write 4 options per question with quality distractors
- Tag each question with skill/difficulty codes

### Step 6: Answer Key Creation
- Verify correct answers are unambiguous
- Check answer distribution across A-D
- Document text evidence for each correct answer

### Step 7: Cross-Test Comparison
- Compare readability scores with other tests
- Verify similar difficulty distribution
- Check for unintended overlap in vocabulary or content

## PILOT TESTING RECOMMENDATIONS

Once all 8 tests are created:

1. **Initial Review:** Have 2-3 English teachers review each test for:
   - Clarity of questions
   - Appropriateness of content
   - Estimated difficulty

2. **Small-Scale Pilot (n=30-50 per test):**
   - Administer each test to representative sample
   - Analyze item statistics (P-values, discrimination)
   - Identify problematic items
   - Revise as needed

3. **Equivalence Testing:**
   - Use common item equating if possible
   - Compare mean scores across tests
   - Verify standard deviations are similar
   - Adjust difficulty if needed

4. **Reliability Testing:**
   - Calculate Cronbach's Alpha for each test
   - Target: α > 0.85
   - If lower, examine item quality

5. **Validity Evidence:**
   - Compare scores to teacher ratings
   - Correlate with other reading measures
   - Verify diagnostic categories are meaningful
